# review-concurrency-3: let reviews run three at a time instead of one at a time

Task: "Something is blocking our process and making a run in a one at a time basis or something" — reviews should run "three at a time".

Done when:
- The cause of the serialization is caught while it happens, with at least five reviews queued, before anything is changed. For each waiting process the evidence includes:
  - the process tree (`pstree -ap`, `ps -eo pid,stat,wchan:32,etime,cmd`)
  - `/proc/<pid>/stack`
  - `py-spy dump --pid <pid>` (or a `gdb` backtrace)
  - `strace -f -tt -e trace=flock,fcntl,futex,wait4,connect`
  - `lsof` output for held lock files and sockets
  - the start and end timestamps of each review from the runner's own log
- The exact lock, flock, single queue consumer, or concurrency/max_workers/-j setting that forces one at a time is named, with its file:line.
- A serial baseline is recorded before the fix:
  - an overlap timeline built from the log
  - `hyperfine --runs 3` timing of a batch of 9 reviews
- The fix sets a bounded limit of exactly 3 concurrent reviews, not an unbounded one.
- `pidstat -u -d 1` was watched during the batch and shows the machine can sustain three.
- (a) A test launches 5 stub reviews and asserts that max overlap == 3. It fails before the fix and passes after.
- (b) The log timeline for 9 real reviews shows 3 running at once and never more than 3.
- (c) `hyperfine` shows the 9-review batch at no more than ~3.3× a single review's time, and the spread is reported.
- (d) The hand-off gives the user a command to start a batch themselves, and running it shows three reviews in progress at once.

Where: the review runner, meaning the jstack Python commands, skills and playbooks that queue and launch reviewers, along with its concurrency setting and lock or queue code, the runner's log, and the tests for the runner.

Do not touch: nothing changes until the serialization has been caught and cited. Do not remove the limit or make it unbounded. Leave the scout, crewmate and home-thread launching alone unless the evidence shows the same lock is what serializes reviews.

Decisions: "three at a time". The user also asked for "a bounded limit of exactly 3 concurrent reviews, not an unbounded one". And "before changing anything, catch the serialization while it happens."

Design: none. The fix is a single bounded concurrency limit, put at the place the investigation cites.
