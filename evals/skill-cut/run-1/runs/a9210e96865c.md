### Intent
> Section 4 of the /deeper design: add a `Proves it:` line to `templates/brief.md`, right after `Done when:`. The brief parser and jbus's pre-launch checks (beside `dispatch.designed`) accept it only when it names something outside the agent: a test, metric, dataset, external judge, or the user. They refuse empty lines, the template's own text, and proofs by the crew, crewmate, reviewer, agent, "self", "the model", or a review alone, with one line saying why. Briefs without the line are refused or grandfathered without stranding an approved row. Tests cover every accepted and refused kind. The home skill's brief section and `docs/` name the line. `make check` and `jgauge test-slot -- make check` pass.

### Reviewers
- Reviewer A: 7 findings
- Reviewer B: 6 findings
- Both reviewers independently raised the same core defect (loose `_OUTSIDE` patterns checked before `_SELF`). They also overlapped on the duplicated head loop, the unused `Brief.proof`, the `<` template detection, and the Result-only Done-when items. The review skill's questions were asked and answered.

### Act On

**1. Self-referential proofs pass because loose `_OUTSIDE` keywords win before `_SELF` is read** (A1, B1, B2)
`src/jbriefs/parse.py` `_OUTSIDE` patterns, used in `proof()`.

Both reviewers found concrete strings that the task says must be refused but that pass today:
- "make sure the reviewer is happy"
- "self-test"
- "`the crewmate` checks it" and other backticked words
- "the reviewer is 100% sure"
- "the model is the judge"
- "the reviewer gives his word"

This is the whole point of the change, and per B1 `proven()` is the only gate before `_ready` launches. The fix is to make each outside pattern specific:
- a test path, test name, or a backticked span that looks like a command or path
- `make <target>` but not "make sure"
- `%` only near a metric word
- drop the bare "his word/pick/eye" and "judge" triggers, or require that something outside remains after the `_SELF` matches are removed.

B2's point belongs to the same fix. The tests only cover the happy side of the precedence rule. Add `REFUSED` cases that run `parse.proof` on each near-miss above, so the tests execute the risky path.

**2. Grandfathering covers every brief without the line, forever** (A2)
`src/jbus/dispatch.py` `proven()` returns `""` whenever `proves_it` returns None.

The Done-when asked that already queued briefs not be stranded. As written, any brief written after this change can skip the line and launch with no gate. The requirement then holds only through prose in the home skill, which is exactly the "rule in prose" failure. The docs sentence "a brief with no `Proves it:` line was written before the line was asked for" is assumed, never checked. The fix is to tie the exemption to something the queue already records (approved time or `brief_sha` before a cut-off) and gate a missing line on anything approved later. It is in `dispatch.py`, inside **Where**.

### Consider

- **One gate, not two, when a brief fails both the design and proof checks** (A3). `dispatch.py:332-334` runs `proven()` only when `designed()` passes, so a brief failing both costs the user two edit-and-approve rounds. Collecting both faults into one gate keyed by the brief sha is a few lines and also removes the `short, kind` juggling.
  - Tradeoff: this only bites briefs that fail both checks, which should be rare. But every round costs the user's time, so it is cheap to fix now.
- **`Brief.proof` (and `Proof.said`) have no consumer outside tests** (A5, B4). The change added surface nothing in `src/` reads.
  - Tradeoff: either drop it or show it on the jbriefs page. Dropping it is the simpler choice unless showing it was planned.
- **Template text detected by a leading `<`** (A6, B5). B5 has a concrete false refusal: `<= 50 ms p95 from jgauge` is a real metric. A6 has the converse: the bracket-stripped guide text passes as "a test".
  - Tradeoff: matching the placeholder pattern `^<[^>]*>$`, or comparing against the template's actual line, is a one-line change. Worth doing alongside Act On #1, since the test cases are being touched anyway.

### Noted

- **Duplicated head-line loop and the `proven` pass-through** (A4, B3). `proves_it` repeats the loop in `designed`. A shared `head_line(text, label)` helper in `jbriefs.parse` is the right shape eventually. Moving `designed` is outside **Where** and its rules are "do not touch", so this is for later. If Consider A3 is taken, its fault list already gives `_ready` the iterate-over-checks shape B3 wants.
- **Result-only Done-when items** (A7, B6). The grandfather-or-refuse statement in the Result, and the real queued brief run through the launch check with the line printed, cannot be seen in a diff. Check them in the Result before accepting. Act On #2 will also change what the Result has to say about grandfathering.

### Dismissed

None. Every finding was either concrete or a reasonable small cleanup. Neither reviewer inflated style preferences.

### Agreement Map

The reviewers converged strongly. Both independently found the `_OUTSIDE`-before-`_SELF` bypass with overlapping and distinct examples. A leaned on "his word" and "self-test"; B on percentages and "judge". That consensus, plus concrete strings that defeat the stated rule, makes it the clear blocker. Their nit-level findings were also the same set: duplicated loop, unused property, `<` detection, and the Result-only items. That suggests the rest of the change is sound.

They diverged once in a way that matters: only A noticed that grandfathering is unbounded, and only A noticed the two-gate sequence. B framed the dispatch side purely as structure (B3). So B read `dispatch.py` for shape, while A read it for behaviour over time, and A's reading surfaced the second real defect.

The pattern: the parser's matching is too permissive and the exemption is too broad. Both are failures of the same kind, because each lets a brief through without naming outside proof. Fixing those two items is what makes the gate real.
