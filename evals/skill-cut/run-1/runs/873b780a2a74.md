### Act On

**1. The step-5 catch-up contradicts "One tick" step 4.** Raised by A1 and B1.
- Step 5 still says "Work each tick as 'One tick' says". That section's step 4 limits the reply to this tick's changes, "Nothing that an earlier message said".
- Ketchup covers everything since the user's last message.
- Autopilot runs while the user is away, so each hourly reply would repeat every earlier tick and grow each hour.
- This is a concrete path, not a hypothetical. The home now has two rules for one reply and nothing to choose between them.
- **Fix within Where:** scope the catch-up in step 5 to "what this tick changed, nothing an earlier message said".
- **Note for later (outside Where):** the full fix is to make "One tick" step 4 in `skills/home/queue-and-its-hourly-audit.md` point to ketchup. B notes that upstream's One tick has no separate rule, which is why upstream avoids the conflict.

**2. The Hand over account conflicts with ketchup's limits.** Raised by A3 and B2.
- Both Hand over lines require a full per-entry account, with links, states, pull requests and "what merged, with the commit".
- Ketchup says to stay under 150 words, leave out hashes, and cut "what happened" before action items.
- A queue of any real size cannot satisfy both, and step 7 of autopilot-full requires every entry to be accounted for.
- Both reviewers found this independently, and it is a correctness problem for the handover.
- **Fix:** add one clause saying the listed account is kept whole and is not cut for length or hashes, and that ketchup governs the voice and the rest.
- This adds jstack wording beyond the port. That is justified, because jstack's step 7 is the local form the audit keeps.

**3. "Changed something *for the user*" leaves a third case.** Raised by A2 and B3.
- The task names two cases: changed something, or changed nothing.
- The added "for the user" creates a third: a tick that launched a crewmate or picked up a stuck thread.
- Combined with ketchup's "leave out work that changed nothing for the user", such a tick can end silently. One tick step 4 explicitly requires reporting launches and stuck threads.
- **Fix:** drop the three words. It is a one-word-level change that makes the text match the "Done when" line literally.

### Consider

**4. No test pins the new behaviour.** Raised by A5 and B4.
- The existing checker executes and confirms that `skills/ketchup/SKILL.md` resolves. Beyond that, nothing would catch a later edit that drops the catch-up or the silent-tick sentence.
- B's version is cheap and executes something: run the existing playbook parser on both files, then assert that step 5 and Hand over reference ketchup and that the no-reply sentence survives.
- A's version, which asserts that two prose rules "agree", is hard to make meaningful. B's version is the one to adopt.
- **Tradeoff:** the change is prose-only, and the stated proof is the drift diff plus the existing tests. A new test is worth it if these playbooks get edited often, but it is not blocking.
