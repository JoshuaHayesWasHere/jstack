### Intent
> Section 3 of the /deeper design: a step inside the home skill's brief writing, not a separate file. It runs on its own for research, perf, analysis and outcome briefs and carries its own copy of the four questions. Answer 2 goes to the scout's `Read:` or the request's sources, answer 3 to the brief for the crew, and answer 4 to `Proves it:`. Mechanical fixes, docs changes and bug fixes with a known cause skip it. `/deeper` stays manual, and `skills/deeper/`, the principle, `templates/brief.md` and the parser are not touched.

### Act On

**1. The step invents an `Instruments:` line and orders a `Read:` line that `templates/brief.md` forbids on home briefs** (A1, B1)
- The template says a home-written brief "leaves all five out", and `Read:` is one of the five.
- `Instruments:` exists nowhere outside this diff. No template, skill, scout shape or parser defines it.
- The phrase "though the home wrote it" admits the clash and leaves it in place.
- The result is that a home reading the template gets the opposite instruction, and a scout following its own skill drops answer 3.
- The intent never asked for a new field. It asked for answer 3 "in the brief for the crew".
- Fix inside "Where": put answer 3 on a line the brief already has, such as the Done-when list or the task body, and say which.
- For answer 2 on a brief the home writes itself, use a place the template allows, not `Read:`. The scout's own brief keeps `Read:` as the intent says.
- If adding `Instruments:` to the template is still wanted, record it in the Result as a follow-up, because `templates/brief.md` is under "Do not touch".

**2. The step is keyed on the playbook name, not on the kind of work** (A2, B2, A3, A4, B3)
- Two reviewers independently traced concrete execution paths.
- `bug-fix` always skips, yet it is the playbook for bugs whose cause is *unknown*. The boundary exempts only a known cause, and the test pins the wrong behaviour under the label "known bug".
- Outcome work under `feature` or `autonomous-run` always skips, so outcome briefs only get the step with no playbook at all. That misses a stated target of the task.
- For scout requests, the scout's playbook decides. Both scout playbooks are in the deeper arm, so a request whose Deliver asks for a known-cause fix or docs brief still gets the four questions. That breaks the second "Done when" line.
- The ten-name list duplicates what each playbook's `Run by:` row already says. A new research playbook skips silently and every test still passes.
- One change fixes all of these: make the kind of work the block's input every time. Use the four kinds plus the skip kinds (mechanical, docs, known-cause fix). The playbook can at most suggest a default.
- For a request, use the kind of the task being briefed, not the scout's playbook.
- Rewrite the skip test to cover a known-cause fix rather than the `bug-fix` playbook. Add an unknown-cause bug case that runs the step.
- A playbook front-matter marker would be the alternative fix, but it touches `playbooks/`, which is outside "Where". Note it for later only if the kind-based input is rejected.

### Consider

**3. A request that delivers no brief drops answers 3 and 4** (A5, B4)
- Answers 3 and 4 are worked out and then have nowhere to go.
- Packing them into the single "Deliver" bullet is also awkward even when a brief is delivered.
- B4's remedy is cheap and uses lines that already exist: write answers 3 and 4 as `Questions:` items in the request.
- Tradeoff: one sentence of text, versus leaving a gap that only matters for report-only requests.
- I lean towards doing it, since you are rewriting line 537 for finding 1 anyway.

**4. No test checks that the lines the step names exist in the templates** (A6)
- The tests do execute the block, so they are not wording-only tests.
- They did, however, let finding 1 through.
- A test that takes each brief line the echoed questions name and asserts it appears in `templates/brief.md` (or `templates/scout-request.md`) would pin the fix for finding 1.
- Tradeoff: it is cheap, but it couples the test to template wording you may change later.
