### Intent
> Add a `Proves it:` line to `templates/brief.md`, right after `Done when:`. The brief parser and jbus's pre-launch checks must accept it only when it names something outside the agent: a test or command, a metric, a dataset, an external judge, or the user. They must refuse it, with a one-line reason, when it is empty, still the template's own text, or a proof by the crew, crewmate, reviewer, agent, "self", "the model" or a review alone. Briefs without the line are refused or grandfathered without stranding an approved row, the choice is stated in the Result, and the change is tested, documented and passes `make check`.

### Reviewers
- Reviewer A: 7 findings
- Reviewer B: 6 findings
- The two agreed on four issues independently: loose `_OUTSIDE` patterns, the duplicated head-line loop, the unused `Brief.proof`, and the `<` template check. Both also flagged that the Result items can't be checked from the diff. The review skill's questions were asked.

### Act On

**1. The `_OUTSIDE` patterns let self-referential proofs through, and the tests don't catch it** (A1, B1, B2)
`proof()` accepts a line as soon as any `_OUTSIDE` pattern matches, and only checks `_SELF` if none did. Several patterns match ordinary words:
- any backticked text
- `make \w`, which matches "make sure"
- `\btests?\b`, which matches "self-test"
- any `%`
- `\bjudge\b`
- `his (word|pick|eye)`
- bare `eval`, `benchmark` and `github`

Each reviewer listed concrete lines that pass, such as "the crewmate will make sure it works", "the reviewer is 100% sure" and "self-test". Every one of them is exactly what the Done-when line says must be refused. Per B1, `_ready` launches whenever `proven()` returns `""`, so this check is the only gate. The suite only tests the happy side of the precedence rule.

Fix: tighten the patterns.
- A test needs a path, a `test_` name, or a backticked command that starts with a known tool.
- `make` needs a real target, not "sure".
- `%` needs a metric word near it.
- Tie "his word" to the user, or drop it.

Then add the reviewers' example lines to `REFUSED` so the suite executes them through `parse.proof`.

**2. Leaving the line out bypasses the gate permanently** (A2)
`proven()` returns `""` whenever there is no `Proves it:` line. That exempts every future brief, not just the ones already queued. The Done-when asks only that approved rows not be stranded. It does not ask that omitting the line be a standing way around the requirement. As written, the requirement holds only through the prose in the home skill.

Fix: grandfather on something the queue already records, such as the row's `approved` time or `brief_sha` before a cut-off, and gate a missing line on anything approved later. It's one reviewer, but the execution path is concrete and it undoes what the user approved.

### Consider

**3. A brief that fails both checks costs two gate-and-approve rounds** (A3)
`proven()` only runs when `designed()` passes. A brief missing both lines therefore needs two edits and two approvals from the user.

Collecting both faults into one gate is a small change in `_ready`, which is in **Where**, and it doesn't touch `designed`'s rules. It's worth doing if it stays that small. The tradeoff is a slightly different gate shape from the one the Design check established.

**4. `Brief.proof` (and `Proof.said`) have no consumer outside the tests** (A5, B4)
This is new surface added by this change with nothing in `src/` reading it. Removing it is cheap and in scope. The alternative is to show it on the jbriefs page, but that wasn't asked for, so removal is the default.

**5. The template text is detected by a leading `<`** (A6, B5)
- B5 is concrete: a real proof like `<= 50 ms p95 from jgauge` is refused as template text.
- A6 is the other side: guide text with the brackets deleted is accepted, because it contains "test".

Matching `^<[^>]*>$`, or comparing against the template's actual line, fixes both and is cheap. The cost is low and so is the impact, which is why this sits in Consider rather than Act On.

### Noted
- **Duplicated head-line loop, and the `_ready` branch pattern** (A4, B3). `proves_it` mirrors `designed`'s loop. A shared `head_line` helper is reasonable, but moving `designed` is outside **Where** and under "Do not touch", so it's for later. Turning `_ready` into a tuple of `(kind, check)` pairs is premature with only two checks. If item 3 is taken, that change subsumes most of it anyway.
- **Result-only Done-when items** (A7, B6). The grandfather-or-refuse statement and the real queued brief run through the launch check, with its line printed, live in the Result, not the diff. Verify them there. Item 2 may also change what the Result has to say.

### Dismissed
- None outright. Every finding was either real or a scope-appropriate note.

### Agreement Map
Both reviewers converged on the same central weakness: the accept-first precedence combined with over-broad keywords. They even gave overlapping example phrases. That is a strong consensus signal on the riskiest part of the change. They also matched on the three smaller structural items, which suggests those are real but minor.

The divergences were one-reviewer catches, and both were substantive:
- A alone saw that a missing line is exempt forever, which is a gap in the intent rather than a style issue.
- A alone saw the double-gate cost to the user.

B added the sharper test-coverage framing and a concrete false-refusal case for the `<` check.

Overall, the parser's shape is fine. Its vocabulary and its handling of a missing line need tightening before it can act as the gate it was meant to be.
