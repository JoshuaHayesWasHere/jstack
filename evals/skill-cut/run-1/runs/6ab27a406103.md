### Intent
> Section 3 of the /deeper design: a step in the home skill's brief writing, not a separate file. It runs on its own for research, perf, analysis and outcome briefs and carries its own copy of the four questions. `/deeper` stays manual. Answer 2 goes to the scout's `Read:` or the request's sources, answer 3 goes in the brief for the crew, and answer 4 goes in `Proves it:`. A mechanical fix, a docs change and a bug fix with a known cause skip the step. The step is pinned by the home skill's tests, and `make check` and `jgauge test-slot -- make check` pass.

### Reviewers
- Reviewer A: 6 findings
- Reviewer B: 7 findings
- Both reviewers answered the review skill's questions. They agree on the two central defects and differ on the edges.

### Act On

**1. Answer 3 goes to an `Instruments:` line nothing defines, and `Read:` contradicts the brief template.** (A1, B1)
- Both reviewers found this independently and confirmed it with a grep: `Instruments:` exists only in this diff.
- `templates/brief.md:17` tells the home to leave `Read:` out of its own briefs, and the home is told to read that template first.
- So a home gets two opposite instructions. A scout following `skills/scout/SKILL.md:125` drops answer 3 entirely. The phrase "though the home wrote it" names the clash without resolving it.
- Within "Where", map answer 3 to a line that already exists: a sentence in the task body, or "Done when" items naming the instrument and its command. Say so in the step.
- The template and scout-skill change (a real `Instruments:` line, and `Read:` on home briefs) goes in the Result as a follow-up, because `templates/brief.md` is under "Do not touch".

**2. The case keys on the playbook name, not the kind of work.** (A2, B2, A3, A4, B3, merged: one root cause)
- The work kind (`research | perf | analysis | outcome`) is only consulted when the playbook is `none`. That causes four concrete misfires:
  - `bug-fix` always skips, though its playbook covers bugs whose cause is unknown. The principle exempts only a proven cause. The test at `tests/test_home_deeper.py:163` locks this in by calling it "known bug".
  - Outcome work under `feature` or `autonomous-run` always skips. The intent explicitly asks for the step on outcome briefs, so this misses a "Done when" line.
  - Research or perf under general playbooks like `swarm` or `arena` skips.
  - For a scout request (`SKILL.md:430`), the scout's own playbook (`investigation` or `trace-forensics`) is always in the deeper arm. So every request gets the step, even one whose Deliver asks for a brief for a refactor or a known-cause fix. That breaks the skip boundary for scout-written briefs.
- The hand-kept list of ten names also drifts. A new research playbook skips silently, and the test checks only one direction.
- The fix removes complexity rather than adding it:
  - Make the kind of work of the task being briefed the block's input in every case. The playbook can suggest a default but never decides.
  - The ten-name list and its one-directional test go away, replaced by tests on the kinds.
  - For a request, the input is the kind of the task the Deliver brief is for, not the scout's playbook.
- A playbook front-matter marker is the alternative. It touches `playbooks/`, so it is noted for later and not needed if the step keys on kind.

### Consider

- **Requests whose Deliver asks for no brief lose answers 3 and 4.** (A5, B4) Answers 3 and 4 are worked out and then go nowhere, and "name them on that line" is an ad hoc way to pack them into a path bullet. B4's remedy is cheap and uses an existing field: write 3 and 4 as `Questions:` items in the request. The tradeoff is small. It is worth doing alongside fix 1, since that touches the same sentence at `:537`.
- **No test checks that the named destination lines exist.** (A6) The current tests run the block, which is fine. A test asserting that each line the echoed questions name appears in `templates/brief.md` would have caught fix 1 and is an executing test. It is worth adding once fix 1 settles which lines are named. If the step stops naming template lines, it is moot.
- **The "Done when already names its data, instruments and judge" skip is prose that overrides the block's output.** (B6) The block prints `deeper` and then a sentence says to ignore it. Once the block keys on kind (fix 2), this can be one more input value and the override disappears. The cost is low only if done with fix 2.

### Noted

- **The pointer "the block under it" at `SKILL.md:430` refers to a block about 90 lines away in another section.** (B5) Name the section. This line is being edited for fix 2 anyway.
- **Nothing in the diff shows `make check` or `jgauge test-slot -- make check` passing.** (B7) The reviewers were told not to run anything, so this is a reporting gap, not a code defect. The Result must state both outcomes with output. These two "Done when" lines stay unmet until it does.

### Dismissed

None. No finding was wrong or a pure preference.

### Agreement Map

The two reviewers converged independently on the two defects that matter:
- the step writes to a line that doesn't exist and a line the template forbids;
- the dispatch keys on the wrong thing.

That consensus, each time with a concrete execution path (`picked='bug-fix'` prints the known-cause skip), is the strongest signal in the review.

They diverged only in coverage:
- A alone saw the scout-request variant of the keying bug.
- B alone saw the general-playbook misses, the prose-only skip, the dangling pointer and the unverified checks.

All of these fold into the same two fixes. The pattern says the core design is sound: its own copy of the four questions, `/deeper` untouched, and the 2/3/4 mapping. Only the input the step reads and the lines it writes to are wrong. Keying on kind and reusing existing brief lines should resolve nearly everything here and shrink the step.
